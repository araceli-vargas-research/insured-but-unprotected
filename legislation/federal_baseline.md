# Federal Baseline and ERISA Boundary

State-law treatment must be measured relative to federal requirements already applicable to the plan or issuer.

## Claims and appeals

- ACA section 2719, codified at [42 U.S.C. § 300gg-19](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid:USC-prelim-title42-section300gg-19), establishes internal claims-and-appeals and external-review requirements for covered plans and issuers.
- [45 C.F.R. § 147.136](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-45/subtitle-A/subchapter-B/part-147/section-147.136) implements ACA internal claims, appeals, and external review rules.
- [29 C.F.R. § 2560.503-1](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-29/subtitle-B/chapter-XXV/subchapter-H/part-2560/section-2560.503-1) governs ERISA benefit-claim procedures.

## ERISA preemption

[29 U.S.C. § 1144](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid:USC-prelim-title29-section1144) contains ERISA's preemption, savings, and deemer provisions. State insurance laws generally regulate insurers and fully insured products, while self-funded employee benefit plans generally cannot be deemed insurers for direct state insurance regulation. Applicability and preemption must be assessed provision by provision; `self-funded ERISA = exempt` is a useful empirical boundary, not a substitute for legal analysis.

## Federal prior-authorization rule

[CMS-0057-F](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/key-initiatives/burden-reduction/interoperability/policies-and-regulations/cms-interoperability-and-prior-authorization-final-rule-cms-0057-f) imposes prior-authorization process and interoperability requirements on specified federally regulated payers, with provisions taking effect on different schedules. It does not create a uniform baseline for every commercial plan. The audit should record whether a state provision adds to, overlaps with, or falls outside this federal rule.

## Empirical implication

The treatment variable should represent the incremental obligation created by state law for the relevant market. Federal changes common to states belong in the time controls or separate federal-policy variables, not in the state-law indicator.

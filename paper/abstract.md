# Insured but Unprotected: State Regulation and Hidden Contract Quality in U.S. Health Insurance

Why does private health insurance in the United States sometimes fail to provide predictable coverage, and can state consumer protections improve insurance quality without increasing premiums or reducing plan choice? Consumers select plans using observable features such as premiums and deductibles but have much less information about the likelihood that an insurer will deny a claim, require prior authorization, or impose a burdensome appeals process. These practices constitute a less visible dimension of insurance-contract quality, raising concerns about whether promised coverage can be enforced after enrollment.

Existing research examines claims denials, prior authorization, insurance-choice frictions, price transparency, and ERISA preemption largely as separate phenomena. Treatment-specific studies evaluate selected prior-authorization prohibitions in isolation, while broader comparisons of state policy remain primarily descriptive. This paper attempts to address that gap by constructing a state-year panel of laws governing prior authorization, denial explanations, appeals, clinical review, and continuity of care. These measures are linked to CMS issuer- and plan-level data on claims denials, appeals, reversal rates, premiums, and Marketplace participation.

Using the staggered implementation of state protections, the paper estimates their effects on insurer behavior and market outcomes. The analysis tests whether these rules reduce denials and make adverse coverage decisions easier to contest, or instead raise premiums and reduce insurer participation by constraining utilization management. A complementary analysis combines Form 5500 filings with state-level MEPS Insurance Component estimates to approximate the prevalence of self-funded employer coverage and assess the practical reach of state consumer protections. Ultimately, this paper evaluates whether state regulation can improve the enforceability of private insurance contracts and at what cost.
